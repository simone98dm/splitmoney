# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Small groups settling one shared occasion: a dinner, a weekend away, a group
gift. Somebody opens the app at the table, enters what was paid, and everyone
leans in to see who owes what. The session lasts about half an hour, then the
app is closed and not reopened until the next occasion — which may be months
later.

That rhythm is the defining constraint. Nobody is a returning power user. Every
visit is effectively a first visit, by someone who is mildly impatient and
surrounded by other people waiting on the answer.

The person holding the phone wants their own number first: **what do I owe, or
what am I owed.** The group total matters second.

## Product Purpose

Settle a shared event exactly, with as few payments as possible. The app takes
a pile of "who paid for what" and returns the shortest list of transfers that
clears every debt to the cent.

Success is the moment the group stops arguing and starts paying — and, shortly
after, an app that visibly declares itself finished.

## Positioning

The expense app whose only job is to end the event. Not a ledger, not a budget
tracker, not a running balance between friends: a calculation that reaches a
final state and gets out of the way.

## Brand Personality

Direct and legible at a glance. The figures are large enough to read from
across a table, the hierarchy is blunt, and there is no decoration competing
with the numbers. Plain-spoken Italian, no financial vocabulary — people are
splitting a dinner, not reconciling accounts.

## Anti-references

Not a bank or fintech app: no navy-and-gold gravitas, no statement-speak, none
of the institutional seriousness that makes splitting a pizza feel like a wire
transfer.

Not a spreadsheet: no dense tables, no zebra rows, no grid of cells. The output
must never look like something you exported.

## Design Principles

**The number before the interface.** The figure someone came for is legible
before any chrome around it is. If a decision makes the number smaller or later,
the decision is wrong.

**Assume no memory.** Design for a person whose last visit was three months ago.
Nothing may depend on remembering how it worked, and no affordance may need
discovering twice.

**Events end.** The app has a finished state and must show it. Reaching zero is
the goal, not an empty list.

**Every figure is traceable.** No amount appears without the user being able to
see what produced it. A settlement nobody can verify is a settlement nobody
trusts.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Note that Use of Color (1.4.1) is Level A and therefore already in
scope: debt and credit must stay distinguishable without color, through sign,
label, and position.
